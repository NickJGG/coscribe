# The Relief Boat

<!-- coscribe:outline -->

Characters: Marta Vell, Oskar Brine

## Summary

Marta arrives on Gullstone Rock to relieve a keeper who isn't there to be relieved. Oskar, the assistant, insists nothing is wrong.

## Foreshadowing and setups

The logbook's last three entries are in a different hand. The fog bell has been muffled with a blanket.

<!-- /coscribe:outline -->

The relief boat left Marta on the landing stage with two crates, a sack of flour and the distinct impression that the boatman had not wanted to stay a minute longer than he had to.

Gullstone Rock rose out of the grey water like a knuckle. The tower stood on top of it, white once, now the colour of old teeth. Somewhere above her the lamp turned in daylight, which it had no business doing.

"You'll be Vell," said a voice. A young man stood on the steps cut into the rock, a lantern in one hand although it was barely past noon. "I'm Brine. Oskar. The assistant."

"Where's Keeper Hale?"

Oskar looked at the sea as if it might answer for him. "Asleep," he said. "He keeps odd hours."

* * *

That night Marta read the logbook by the light of the stove. Wind, weather, ships sighted, oil burned. Hale's handwriting was cramped and careful. Then, three days ago, it changed: the letters grew tall and looped, and the entries stopped mentioning ships at all.

_Lamp lit at dusk. The light is watching._

# The Fog Bell

Fog came in on the third morning, thick enough to lose your own hands in. Marta climbed to the gallery to ring the bell and found it wrapped in a grey wool blanket, tied off with fishing line.

She cut the line with her pocketknife. The blanket fell away into the fog and did not, as far as she could hear, land anywhere.

"Don't," said Oskar from the stairwell. He was holding the lantern again. "He doesn't like the bell."

"Hale?"

Oskar didn't answer. Out on the water something large moved against the current, and the lamp above them stopped turning.

# The Keeper's Room

The door at the top of the tower had been painted shut. Marta found that out with her shoulder, twice, before she found the key on a nail behind the oil drums, hung exactly where a careful man would hang it.

Inside, the room was cold and neat. The bed was made. On the desk lay Hale's spectacles, folded, and a chart of the Gullstone shoals with one rock circled over and over until the pencil had gone through the paper.

The circled rock had no name on the chart. Marta knew every rock in these waters by name. This one had not been there last spring.

# The Long Night

They kept watch together, because neither of them would admit to being afraid of keeping it alone. Oskar trimmed the wick. Marta wrote the log in her own hand, plainly, the way Hale used to.

_Lamp lit at dusk. Wind north-east, moderate. One ship sighted, the_ Brightwater_, bound south._

Near midnight the beam swept the circled rock and, for the length of one turn, someone stood on it, waving a lantern.
